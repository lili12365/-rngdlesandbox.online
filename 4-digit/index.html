Rules for a 4-digit number game

One four-digit number is selected from 0001 to 9999. Enter a valid whole number and use the response to adjust your next guess. A lower guess means the answer is higher; a higher guess means the answer is lower. Keep leading zeroes when you display the value, so 42 is written as 0042 in a four-digit format.

You can approach the game as a logic exercise. Keep a mental or written lower bound and upper bound, and never make a guess outside that interval. A good guess does not need to be clever. It only needs to make the remaining search smaller.

Worked example

Hidden answer: 6842

Guess 5000: too low, so keep 5001-9999.

Guess 7500: too high, so keep 5001-7499.

Guess 6250: too low, so keep 6251-7499.

Guess 6875: too high, then move to the midpoint of 6251-6874. The interval keeps shrinking until 6842 is the only sensible target.

This example shows why alternating large jumps with tiny guesses is inefficient. The midpoint gives you the most information per attempt.

Strategy for four digits

The full range contains 9,999 possible values. A binary-search rhythm can reduce that range quickly: begin around 5000, then move to the midpoint after every higher or lower clue. If the game also gives a close signal, use it as a transition point. Once you are within a small distance, stop making broad jumps and test nearby values.

  • Use 5000 as a neutral opener.
  • Record the current minimum and maximum after every guess.
  • Use 2500 or 7500 as the next anchor, depending on direction.
  • Format short values with leading zeroes: 7 becomes 0007.

FAQ

Are leading zeroes allowed?

Yes. The display format is always four digits, so values below 1000 include leading zeroes.

What is the fastest way to solve it?

Binary search is the strongest general strategy because each midpoint eliminates about half of the remaining values.

Where can I play a daily version?

Practice notes for four-digit guessing

The four-digit format becomes much easier when you treat it as a record-keeping exercise. Before entering a guess, identify the lowest and highest possible value. After the response, update one boundary and discard everything outside it. This is faster than remembering a loose impression such as "somewhere around the high six-thousands."Exact boundaries make your next midpoint obvious.

Leading zeroes are a display convention, not extra difficulty. The value 42 and the four-digit string 0042 represent the same number, but writing 0042 keeps the format consistent. When sharing a result, include the mode name so a friend knows whether you solved a 1-100, four-digit, or five-digit puzzle.

Players who already use binary search can estimate how many candidates remain. If the range is 6251-7499, 6875 is a stronger choice than 6800 because it divides that set more evenly. This turns intuition into a measurable decision. It also helps explain why a midpoint is useful even when it is not a round number.

Replay a solved round without looking at the answer and compare each move to the ideal midpoint. The goal is not to memorize a sequence, but to notice where a guess failed to split the remaining range efficiently. If your first guess was too high, write down the exact upper bound before continuing. A precise boundary is more valuable than a vague sense of direction.

You can add difficulty by limiting the number of attempts, hiding the close-range clue, or asking a second player to justify each guess before it is entered. You can also alternate between a four-digit game and the smaller 1-100 guessing game to see how the same strategy scales. When you are ready for a shared scheduled challenge, play the daily number guessing game.

A good four-digit round should feel challenging but explainable. At the end, you should be able to point to every clue and show how it changed the next interval. That is the lasting skill behind the game: turning a broad set of possibilities into one defensible answer.

Improve your next four-digit round

After a round, review the sequence rather than only the final score. Ask whether your first guess divided the range, whether you updated the correct boundary, and whether you repeated a value that had already been ruled out. This review takes less than a minute and builds a stronger habit than simply trying another random target.

A useful exercise is to say the interval aloud before every entry. If the answer is between 5101 and 7499, explain why 6300 is a better candidate than 7000: it leaves a more balanced number of possibilities on either side. This practice makes the strategy clear even when the final answer is not found immediately.

Keep the format consistent

Agree before starting whether answers are displayed with leading zeroes and whether a close clue is active. Consistent formatting makes shared results comparable and keeps the focus on deduction rather than interpretation.
